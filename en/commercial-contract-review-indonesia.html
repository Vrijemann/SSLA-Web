When your contract needs a review

  • You have received a draft from the other side and are being asked to sign quickly.
  • The contract commits significant value or runs for a long term.
  • Your counterparty is Indonesian and you are not, or the contract exists only in English.
  • You are still using an old template that has never been updated.
  • A clause feels one-sided, but you are not sure how much risk it carries.

Contracts with foreign parties

Indonesian must be used in agreements involving an Indonesian private entity or an Indonesian citizen. Where a foreign party is involved, the agreement must also be written in that party's national language and/or English (Article 31(1) and (2) of Law No. 24 of 2009). For that reason we prepare bilingual contracts from the outset, together with an agreed position on which version prevails if the two are read differently.

We also look closely at the governing-law and dispute-resolution clauses. If you choose arbitration outside Indonesia, the award will need an exequatur from the Chief Judge of the Central Jakarta District Court before it can be enforced here (Articles 65 and 66 of Law No. 30 of 1999). That is better known at signing than discovered at enforcement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a contract valid if it is not executed before a notary?

Generally, yes. The requirements for a valid agreement (Article 1320 of the Indonesian Civil Code) do not call for a notarial deed, except for particular agreements that the law requires to be made by authentic deed. The form of the deed does, however, affect its evidentiary weight.

The contract has been signed. Can it still be changed?

Yes, through an addendum agreed by both parties. A lawfully made agreement binds the parties as if it were law, and may not be revoked except by mutual consent or on grounds provided by law (Article 1338 of the Civil Code).

Can a contract with an Indonesian party be in English only?

Where one of the parties is an Indonesian private entity or citizen, Indonesian must be used (Article 31 of Law No. 24 of 2009). We recommend a bilingual text from the start, with an agreed clause on which version prevails.
