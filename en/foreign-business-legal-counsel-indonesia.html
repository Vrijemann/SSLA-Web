Foreign workers: compliance and disputes

We focus on the legal side of employing foreign workers (tenaga kerja asing, or TKA): making sure the employer's obligations are met, drafting the employment contracts, and handling issues as they arise. These are the core obligations under Law No. 13 of 2003 on Manpower, as amended by Law No. 6 of 2023.

Obligation or prohibitionBasis
An employer of foreign workers must hold a foreign worker utilisation plan (RPTKA) approved by the Central GovernmentArticle 42(1)
Individual employers may not employ foreign workersArticle 42(2)
Foreign workers may be employed only in an employment relationship, for a specific position and a specific period, and must have competence matching the positionArticle 42(4)
Foreign workers may not hold positions dealing with personnel (HR) mattersArticle 42(5)
The employer must appoint an Indonesian counterpart, provide training, and repatriate the foreign worker when employment endsArticle 45(1)
The employer must pay a compensation fee for each foreign worker employedArticle 47(1)
A fixed-term employment contract must be in Indonesian; if it is bilingual and the versions differ, the Indonesian text prevailsArticle 57(1) and (2)

Breaches of the RPTKA and compensation obligations may attract administrative sanctions (Article 190(1)), while breaches of the counterpart, training, and repatriation obligations carry criminal sanctions (Article 187(1)). More detailed rules are set out in Government Regulation No. 34 of 2021 on the Utilisation of Foreign Workers.

Frequently asked questions

Can an employment contract with a foreign worker be in English only?

No. A fixed-term employment contract must be in Indonesian. A bilingual text is allowed, but if the versions differ, the Indonesian text prevails (Article 57(1) and (2)).

Do foreign directors or commissioners also need an RPTKA?

The RPTKA requirement does not apply to directors or commissioners holding a certain level of shares, or to shareholders, in accordance with the applicable regulations (Article 42(3)(a)). Whether it applies should be checked case by case.

What happens if a foreign worker's contract is ended early?

A party that ends a fixed-term employment relationship before the agreed term, other than on grounds provided by law, must pay the other party compensation equal to the worker's wages for the remainder of the term (Article 62 of Law No. 13 of 2003).
